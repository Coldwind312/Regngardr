Step aboard the good ship *Least Weasel* and travel the spaceways with Captain Myriad Issigri.

*Starfield* is a science-fiction roleplaying game from Bethesda—them as did *Skyrim*. And in many ways it is, pretty much, *Skyrim* in space. You set up a character and find yourself in a fixed starting position. In *Skyrim*, this was the cart carrying you and other prisoners to your collective execution. In *Starfield*, you're a grunt miner working for an extraction company.

In *Skyrim*, your impending dispatch from this world (that world, at least) is interrupted by the arrival of a **DRAGON!** which sets about torching the town you're in, and you leg it through some underground catacombs to approximate safety. After which your job is to go meet this dude in a nearby village and follow a central plotline whilst carrying out side quests raiding dungeons and looting abandoned old castle and stuff.

The nice thing about *Skyrim* is that if you wanted to you could more or less ignore that central plotline. Which I duly did. There's enough to do in the open-world sections of the game to not really bother that much about being pulled along on rails through a story. I tend to prefer games that give me an environment and let me make my own way through it: think 1984's *Elite* and its sequels if you want my ideal game structure. *Skyrim* isn't that, quite: though it gives a pretty decent amount of freedom, you're still conscious of the story you're avoiding.

But honestly, though I've always tended to grate against games that make me follow a specific storyline, my experience playing *Cyberpunk 2077* has kind of put a dent in that certainty. That game gave me the story of V, a young street mercenary in the grimly and grimily futuristic Night City, who embarks on a risky heist to cement her[^1] name amongst NC's underworld legends, and when the heist goes catastrophically wrong, spends the rest of the game trying to avoid a technologically induced doom.

*Cyberpunk's* story, it turned out, absolutely gripped me. Between the engaging supporting characters and the intriguing premise and the presence of **Actual Keanu Reeves** (the *Phantom Liberty* expansion also brings us **Actual Idris Elba** and it's a shame that expansion didn't grip me anything like as much), I couldn't put it down until the end. And there are several ends, depending on choices you've made through the game.

Point is, I'm not perhaps as averse to narrative play as I once was, though I still prefer games like *Skyrim* and *Cyberpunk* where you can just wander off and do your own thing for a while, and come back to the storyline when you feel like it.

In *Starfield* you can very much do that. You can essentially treat the central plotline as a hobby: come back to it as and when you want to; forget about it entirely for long periods if you like; give it up and find another one.

[^1]: V's gender is optional, so you'll hear people referring to V as she, or he, or they, and these options are just as valid. I usually play the she version, though, and that tends to be how I think about her.
